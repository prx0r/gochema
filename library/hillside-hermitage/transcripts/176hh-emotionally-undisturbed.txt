and sense objects)?

three thresholds or domains that your mind might be living on:
 
 
"Bhava: being, “becoming”, “process of existence”, consists of 3 planes (Cf. loka):
 
sensuous existence (kāma-bhava),
 
fine-material existence (rūpa-bhava),
 
immaterial existence (arūpa-bhava)."

If you're not free from sensuality, your mind is fully within the being of sensuality. That means
 
being within senses and sense objects, as your basis, as your world. That is your extent. So
 
from that point of view, you can't experience any form of emotion, any form of feeling, that will
 
not be rooted within or upon that very threshold of the senses.
 
 
 
Any form of disturbance, even if it's just a mental idea of something, it's always going to boil
 
down to something you either see, hear, smell, taste or touch. And when you think about it,
 
when you look at the content of those thoughts that are now causing a disturbance, it's because
 
they imply some disagreeable sight, smell, sound, taste, or touch. From this point of view, even
 
the thoughts are within that sensual domain and their content is still sense-based.
 
 
The reason why that is practically relevant is because any emotional discomfort always feels like
 
it's much more. But if you were to start seeing it correctly, you realise that in its heart it's just an
 
unpleasant sight, a smell and so on. It always feels much more because you're always trying to
 
get rid of it at face value. You imply there is more to it than there is. That's why for an Arahant
 
who is fully freed from any craving for or craving against anything that arises on the level of
 
feeling, in the seen, there is just the seen, in the heard, there is just the heard, there isn't
 
anything else there. And there never was.
 
 
Q: In the pain, there is just the pain.
 
 
2
 
Nm: Yes, from the point of view of feeling, but you also want to see that that pain is rooted upon
 
something of the sense kind, sight, sound, smell, taste, touch. For example, if you experience a
 
disturbance on account of somebody who has died. You will never SEE them again. So there is
 
a change in the domain of sight. Or you will never talk to them again, you will never get to touch
 
them again. It's just deprivation of the senses. Certain perceptions have changed and you
 
cannot experience it in the same way. That form that you used to see of that person, now
 
cannot be seen, and on account of that sight, that has changed, you grieve, you are disturbed,
 
but it's just a sight, and was always just a sight. And now it's just an absence of it.
 
 
 
Would you be able to experience love, infatuation, attachment etc, towards anyone if you've
 
never seen them? If you never heard or touched them? Would you be able to even think about
 
it? No, it's impossible.
 
 
For example, in SN 35.95 the Buddha asks:
 
"What do you think, Malunkyaputta? Objects cognizable by the eye, not seen, which you have not seen before,
 
which you do not see now and do not wish to see — have you any desire, lust and fondness for them?"
 
 
"No indeed, Lord."
 
 
[Similarly for sounds, scents, flavours, tangible things, mental objects.]
 
 
"Well then, Malunkyaputta, in things seen, heard, sensed, cognized: in the seen, there will only be the seen, in
 
the heard only the heard, in the sensed only the sensed, in the cognized only the cognized…"
 
 
You cannot experience lust for things you haven't seen, your lust is fully circumstantial. And if
 
you've seen them, you realise also that you 'could have not' seen them and you 'will not' see
 
them. Which then puts both the object of your senses and your senses back into perspective.
 
 
Q: If you are longing for a sight, do you just say to yourself," It's just a sight" and then the
 
longing will disappear?
 
 
NM: Well, you want to ask why are you saying that to yourself? Because longing is unpleasant.
 
Are you saying "That it's just a sight" so that it helps you get rid of the displeasure? But you
 
shouldn't be trying to get rid of it. The arisen experience is that you're emotionally engaged with
 
the domain of sight. You're longing for it, wanting it, not wanting it, either way, you and the sight
 
are in a relationship, on account of that which you feel.
 
 
You have to accept that as a starting point. So the only thing you must do is understand it. And
 
when I say understand it, I don't mean develop some hypothesis that arises on account of it. I
 
mean, understand the nature of that relationship. What is the nature of longing for a sight? Well,
 
it implies the necessity of your internal sense bases. In this case: your eyes. So it implies the
 
presence of your eyes, that pressure to go and see something you want or the pressure to avoid
 
seeing something you don't want. If you don't have the eyes, none of that's applicable. So part
 
3
 
of the understanding of that relationship is understanding the assumption regarding the eyes.
 
Eye's see and you're subjected to what they see. But do you have control over the organs that
 
are your eyes?
 
 
 
Remind yourself of that context: " These eyes are these balls of flesh in the world on account of
 
which I get sights, that's it. In their form, these organs are no different than other material things
 
found in the world. So, fundamentally I have no control over my eyes, in the sense of, I'm not
 
their owner, I can't prevent them from suddenly not working anymore,or seeing something I
 
don't want to see”. If you were truly the Master, you would only see things you want to see. You
 
would be the master of that which arises on account of the eyes, which are sights. And yet here
 
you are grieving on account of sights you didn't want to see, or sights you wanted to see but
 
couldn't.
 
 
 
So, when we talk about understanding, it's not about discerning some theoretical relationship.
 
It's drilling in, repetitively, the obviousness of the nature of these things. I say the obviousness
 
because it's obvious, and because it's obvious people don't think about it AGAIN. But it's not
 
enough to see that it's obvious, that eyes are not yours, you need to keep repeating that same
 
obvious knowledge for as long as you have lust regarding sights left. If you understood that
 
correctly, it's then impossible to experience lust towards sights or any form of disturbance. It's
 
because you don't see the nature of your senses, because you haven't understood it, that is why
 
you are disturbed. So the obviousness of it should not fool you into thinking "Okay, I got this
 
what next?" Ok, you got it. Does that mean you're free from sensuality? No. Then you have to
 
keep reflecting: “Can a sight disturbed me in the future?”, and so on.
 
 
You can practice, have insights and things can make sense, but that's only part of the picture.
 
The other part is, you keep asking yourself, "Can unarisen sensuality arise in me? Does the
 
arisen sensuality decrease? Is my mind quicker to free itself from lust? Am I free or is this just
 
some kind of a temporary novelty relief?". You want that understanding to also prevent you from
 
being disturbed in the future, not just freeing you from whatever was disturbing you while you
 
were thinking about it.
 
 
You want to start seeing 'your eyes' like a stranger's eyes, the only difference is that 'your' eyes
 
are closest to your point of view than other people's eyes, but either way, eyes are there and
 
sights are there. Your eyes are not more yours than my eyes. They're just closer in their
 
arrangement regarding your point of view, and because of that proximity, one assumes "this is
 
mine", because there's nothing else between me and this, as opposed to there being a lot in
 
between me and someone else's eyes. But between me and my eyes, there's nothing. There is
 
just "I see".
 
 
 
That's why the sense bases are always described in negative terms, as absences, so to speak,
 
they are defined by things you perceive. You can't see your eyes, but you can know that the
 
eyes are there because of what you see.

There's nothing that you can perceive between you and your sense bases. And that's why the
 
ownership of the entire world is rooted upon the ownership of the senses and the body. And you
 
can gradually remove that ownership through seeing that these things operate on their own,
 
They don't even know about you, you don't exist for them. Eyes just see, they seek the
 
agreeable sights and avoid the disagreeable.
 
 
Q: And when you don't own them anymore, when you know their nature, they will still go for
 
what is agreeable.
 
 
Nm: Yes, and avoid what is disagreeable, because agreeable and disagreeable fundamentally
 
do not require lust for its being. However there is no lust without agreeable or disagreeable.
 
Agreeable and disagreeable is what senses do. Through your assumption of ownership
 
regarding the senses, you are then pulled by agreeable and repelled by disagreeable, and then
 
there is lust, there is aversion, there is delusion/distraction. But if you remove the assumed
 
ownership, there is no more lust towards agreeable, there is no more aversion towards
 
disagreeable, and there is no more distraction towards neutral.
 
 
Q: What is then stopping the eye from going wild?
 
 
Nm: The absence of lust. For example, the eye sees an agreeable sight and that's where it
 
stops. It's your mind infected with lust that then wants more, wants to taste it, wants to touch it,
 
wants to have it etc. If it's disagreeable, it wants to destroy it, wants to kill it. Those are not the
 
senses anymore, it's just you lusting and being angry. So there is still the basis of disagreeable
 
and agreeable, and if you have no lust and no aversion, that cannot become more, it remains
 
agreeable or disagreeable.
 
 
Q: So let's say a disagreeable sight arises and I'm not free from suffering...
 
 
Nm: So that's the starting point - the discomfort. The first thing is that you must not act out of
 
trying to get rid of the discomfort because that's going to be acting out of aversion. The only way
 
you can practice the Dhamma is to not be rooted in craving. And if you're trying to get rid of the
 
aversion that has arisen on account of disagreeable sight, you are rooted in craving. Thus, there
 
will be no Dhamma you practice.
 
 
 
So first, what is the present situation for you? Well, it's the unpleasant feeling on account of a
 
disagreeable sight. But then you reflect, "There is discomfort, the senses are subject to
 
discomfort, therefore are senses mine? To what extent are they mine? I'm not sure if it's mine or
 
not? Well, if it's mine, would I be experiencing any discomfort?” etc
 
 
You're not privy to the pathway whereby the body as sense organs experiences corresponding
 
senses objects. All you have is - "eyes meet the sights, somewhere in the world". And then if
 
you are aware of it, you get the corresponding pressure (contact/phassa). The only reason you
 
5
 
know that " eye has met the sight", is because you're already pressured, and on account of the
 
visual pressure, you know that eyes have seen.
 
 
And that "eyes and seeing", you cannot get in between them. Even if you shut your eyelids,
 
you're seeing the inside of your eyelids. Eyes are always seeing. And it's inconceivable for you
 
to prevent eyes from seeing. So how can that be yours in any shape or form, when you don't
 
even know how these things come together? You're only subjected to the resulting pressure of
 
it.
 
 
 
So now as you can see, there are many things to reflect upon correctly, without trying to get rid
 
of that uncomfortable pressure of the disagreeable sight. That's what feeling is - the pressure,
 
pressure towards pleasure, pressure away from pain, pressure towards oblivion.
 
 
Q: The body will still move away from a painful touch even though you no longer assume
 
ownership of it.
 
 
Nm: Yes, and you wouldn't have to overthink it, "Oh, look, the body moved because the knee
 
was hurting". The body doesn't need you. You're invisible to the five aggregates, you don't exist
 
for them. And it's because of that, that becoming an Arahant is possible.
