 YouTube Sutta Nipāta 5.11 Kappamā ṇ avapucchā Sa ṃ yutta Nikāya 22.43 Attadīpasutta 
 
When greed, hatred, and delusion arise, there is a common assumption that these 
states
 
must
 
be
 
prevented
 
from
 
manifesting.
 
You
 
recognize
 
that
 
these
 
things
 
are
 
unwholesome,
 
and
 
because
 
you
 
are
 
unable
 
to
 
prevent
 
them
 
from
 
arising,
 
you
 
believe
 
you
 
are
 
failing
 
or
 
moving
 
backwards.
 
This
 
is
 
the
 
wrong
 
assumption.
 
You
 
are
 
trying
 
to
 
prevent
 
things
 
from
 
manifesting
 
in
 
the
 
first
 
place,
 
but
 
that
 
is
 
not
 
on
 
you.
 
That
 
cannot
 
be
 
on
 
you.
 
When you fail to see where your responsibility lies, you overdo the aspect of trying to 
get
 
rid
 
of
 
unwholesome
 
states.
 
By
 
attempting
 
to
 
prevent
 
them,
 
you
 
bind
 
yourself
 
to
 
them.
 
You
 
become
 
like
 
a
 
dog
 
that
 
runs
 
around
 
a
 
post
 
trying
 
to
 
get
 
away
 
from
 
it;
 
the
 
dog
 
ends
 
up
 
being
 
tied
 
tighter
 
and
 
closer
 
to
 
the
 
post.
 
The manifestation itself is not the problem. The manifestation is not the greed itself; 
it
 
is
 
the
 
prospect
 
of
 
greed
 
being
 
presented.
 
The
 
problem
 
is
 
you
 
acting
 
out
 
of
 
greed
 
or
 
aversion.
 
It
 
is
 
you
 
choosing
 
to
 
act
 
by
 
body,
 
speech,
 
or
 
mind,
 
delighting
 
in
 
the
 
manifestation
 
and
 
welcoming
 
it.
 
That
 
is
 
your
 
doing.
 
If you truly had a say as to what appears and what does not, these things would not 
have
 
arisen
 
in
 
the
 
first
 
place.
 
They
 
arise
 
on
 
their
 
own.
 
You
 
have
 
no
 
say,
 
yet
 
you
 
act
 
as
 
if
 
you
 
do
 
because
 
you
 
think
 
the
 
problem
 
is
 
in
 
the
 
manifestation
 
rather
 
than
 
in
 
your
 
attitude
 
toward
 
it.
 
This
 
is
 
where
 
sense
 
restraint
 
applies.
 
Sense restraint is not preventing things from arising; it’s the abandoning of acting 
out
 
of
 
the
 
craving-pressure
 
towards
 
them.
 
Being
 
restrained
 
means
 
interfering
 
less
 
with
 
the
 
manifestation
 
of
 
things.
 
2 
The Doorway of Choice and Responsibility 
Manifestations cannot burst forth unless you choose to act out of them. The pressure 
to
 
act
 
might
 
be
 
there,
 
but
 
no
 
amount
 
of
 
pressure
 
can
 
make
 
a
 
choice
 
for
 
you.
 
It
 
is
 
always
 
you
 
who
 
chooses
 
to
 
act
 
out
 
of
 
the
 
pressure,
 
which
 
means
 
you
 
can
 
also
 
choose
 
not
 
to
 
act.
 
You are the guardian of the doorway through which the pressure can express itself. 
You
 
are
 
the
 
one
 
who
 
opens
 
the
 
door,
 
or
 
does
 
not.
 
Not
 
knowing
 
that,
 
it
 
feels
 
as
 
if
 
the
 
action
 
of
 
acting
 
out
 
comes
 
automatically
 
out
 
of
 
the
 
pressure.
 
You must discern the line for which you are responsible, the line you do not cross. 
Consider
 
the
 
simile
 
of
 
a
 
town
 
surrounded
 
by
 
a
 
wall
 
with
 
only
 
one
 
point
 
of
 
entry
 
and
 
exit.
 
If
 
you
 
want
 
to
 
keep
 
track
 
of
 
what
 
is
 
going
 
on
 
inside,
 
you
 
do
 
not
 
need
 
to
 
chase
 
people
 
who
 
come
 
in
 
or
 
go
 
after
 
people
 
who
 
go
 
out.
 
You
 
just
 
stay
 
at
 
the
 
doorway.
 
This is the doorway of the mindful context in regard to feelings, perceptions, and 
intentions.
 
It
 
is
 
not
 
a
 
doorway
 
of
 
focusing
 
on
 
the
 
tip
 
of
 
your
 
nose
 
where
 
you
 
are
 
not
 
aware
 
of
 
anything
 
else.
 
That
 
is
 
staring
 
at
 
the
 
woodwork
 
of
 
the
 
doorway
 
without
 
seeing
 
who
 
walks
 
through
 
it.
 
You
 
must
 
see
 
whatever
 
has
 
manifested
 
itself
 
and
 
know
 
it
 
has
 
manifested
 
to
 
this
 
extent.
 
You must take responsibility for your choices. Do not deny the choice in hindsight by 
claiming,
 
“There
 
is
 
no
 
self,
 
so
 
this
 
choice
 
couldn't
 
have
 
been
 
mine.”
 
When
 
you
 
make
 
a
 
choice
 
rooted
 
in
 
greed,
 
aversion,
 
or
 
delusion,
 
there
 
is
 
conceit;
 
there
 
is
 
a
 
sense
 
of
 
“I
 
am.”
 
It
 
is
 
your
 
choice.
 
The
 
choice
 
is
 
always
 
yours.
 
Through that sense of responsibility for the slightest choice you make, you can 
eventually
 
overcome
 
the
 
ownership
 
of
 
the
 
choice
 
and
 
the
 
responsibility
 
with
 
it.
 
You
 
remove
 
the
 
ownership
 
of
 
responsibility
 
by
 
refusing
 
to
 
deny
 
responsibility.
 
Stop engaging with things by trying to prevent the basis for your choices. Discern 
where
 
your
 
doing
 
is,
 
and
 
then
 
stop
 
doing
 
that.
 
 
 
3 
Discerning the Limits of the Shoreline 
When you stop interfering, there is just an arising and passing away, the same as 
waves
 
striking
 
the
 
seashore.
 
When
 
a
 
wave
 
comes
 
in
 
and
 
finally
 
reaches
 
the
 
shoreline,
 
it
 
disintegrates
 
and
 
vanishes.
 
The
 
wave
 
goes
 
no
 
further
 
than
 
the
 
limit
 
of
 
the
 
shoreline.
 
In
 
the
 
same
 
way,
 
nothing
 
can
 
go
 
beyond
 
the
 
limits
 
established
 
by
 
your
 
own
 
awareness.
 
Nothing
 
can
 
enter
 
through
 
that
 
door,
 
because
 
you
 
realize
 
nothing
 
could
 
have
 
ever
 
entered.
 
It
 
was
 
you
 
who
 
was
 
going
 
out
 
through
 
the
 
door
 
and
 
being
 
affected
 
by
 
things
 
outside.
 
It
 
was
 
you
 
stepping
 
from
 
the
 
shore
 
into
 
the
 
water
 
that
 
is
 
hit
 
by
 
the
 
waves.
 
The mind can arise in all shapes and forms, whichever way it wants. That is not the 
problem.
 
You
 
welcoming
 
it,
 
entertaining
 
it,
 
or
 
trying
 
to
 
get
 
rid
 
of
 
it
 
is
 
the
 
problem.
 
If
 
you
 
stop
 
doing
 
that,
 
the
 
basis
 
for
 
acts
 
of
 
greed
 
still
 
arises,
 
but
 
there
 
is
 
no
 
greed.
 
Greed
 
comes
 
from
 
you;
 
it
 
does
 
not
 
come
 
from
 
things.
 
The waves continue to come and go. You do not get wet anymore because you do not 
go
 
outside
 
where
 
the
 
waves
 
are.
 
You
 
do
 
not
 
fight
 
the
 
sea
 
to
 
remain
 
dry;
 
you
 
see
 
the
 
range
 
of
 
the
 
wave
 
and
 
leave
 
it
 
alone.
 
Finding the place where those elements cannot set foot is Nibbāna, the 
unmanifested,
 
the
 
unborn.
 
The
 
island
 
is
 
revealed
 
by
 
the
 
waves.
 
Nibbāna
 
is
 
outlined
 
by
 
the
 
sea;
 
from
 
whatever
 
is
 
manifested,
 
Nibbāna
 
is
 
outlined
 
by
 
being
 
the
 
opposite
 
of
 
that.
 
You recognize that a manifestation cannot be owned because it is anicca. It is dukkha 
because
 
it
 
lacks
 
the
 
pleasure
 
connected
 
with
 
mastery
 
over
 
things.
 
Because
 
of
 
that,
 
it
 
cannot
 
be
 
yours;
 
hence,
 
it
 
is
 
anattā.
 
Human existence is a discrepancy between how things actually are and how you take 
them
 
to
 
be.
 
Any
 
degree
 
of
 
discrepancy
 
determines
 
the
 
degree
 
of
 
liability
 
toward
 
dukkha.
 
Where
 
there
 
is
 
no
 
discrepancy,
 
there
 
is
 
no
 
basis
 
for
 
dukkha.
 
In the seen, there is just the seen. Lust, aversion, and delusion are not in the seen; 
they
 
are
 
in
 
your
 
attitude
 
toward
 
it.
 
Remove
 
the
 
attitude,
 
and
 
you
 
extinguish
 
the
 
discrepancy.

"when
