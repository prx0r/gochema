## STOP 0–1 — the unfairness charge + theodicy (believer voices first)

**Bhikkhu Jayasena (r/Buddhism, QUOTED)** — the birth-lottery candor, in
vernacular: "how you were born is pretty much 100% your kamma. The issue
here is that people have a hard time bridging the gap between this life and
past lives… in all the untold numbers of lives we have been stuck in samsara,
we've all been Hitler, and we've all been mother Theresa."
— https://www.reddit.com/r/Buddhism/comments/ifou1k/ — STOP 1: the domino
stated by a monk, no hedging. Verbatim-public (Reddit).

**Regular_Bee (r/Buddhism, QUOTED)** — "Samsara simply isn't fair, its
terrible that we're subject to karma… Buddhism isn't telling us comforting
things that we want to hear, like that the universe is just. It's telling
us the brutal reality, so we can do something about it."
— https://www.reddit.com/r/Buddhism/comments/10oqhor/ — STOP 1 guard: karma
isn't consolation, it's diagnosis. Verbatim-public.

**Charles Jones / DharmaWheel (QUOTED)** — the Holocaust question, answered
by a scholar: "karma is just cause and effect. It's not moral; there is no
judge and there's no concept of 'deserving'… Perpetrators of cruelty cannot
hide behind karma… whoever did something unskillful in a past life is only
'me' in a very qualified way… that past self is 'me' and 'not-me.'"
— https://www.dharmawheel.net/viewtopic.php?t=43678 — STOP 1: the hardest
case faced head-on; "me and not-me" is the film's distancing device.
Verbatim-public (forum).

**Bodhi Path child-cancer Middle Way (QUOTED)** — "'Karma' refers to
tendencies and habits, not a direct label for single events… The causes of
disease are intertwined with multiple causal conditions, of which karma is
only one type… Buddhist ethics focus on 'how not to cause more harm in the
present' rather than 'seeking to blame someone.'"
— https://bodhi.lawtw.com/en/news/20251211131455bda4e0 — STOP 1: the
believer's theodicy voice the film needs (a sick child is NOT "retribution",
nor "random"). Chinese Buddhist network; verify translation at draft.

**Mikel Burley, "Retributive karma and the problem of blaming the victim"
(LINKED — White Rose eprints PDF, free)** — three criticisms (no practical
guidance; free-will-vs-fatalism dilemma; victim-blaming as morally repugnant),
responses considered, plus the Wittgenstein move: the dispute is a
"disagreement in form of life rather than… opinions." Also: "a morally
significant difference between alternative ways of articulating the belief
in karma."
— https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/85287/ — STOP 1 structure: the
three charges ARE the film's guard chapter; the form-of-life point is the
domino thesis in academic dress. Fetch full text before scripting.

## STOP 1 — the weaponization data (cold-open gold)

**APA 2025, White et al., Psychology of Religion and Spirituality (QUOTED,
press release)** — "69% of participants… assigned to write about themselves
wrote about a positive karmic experience, while only 18% of those assigned
to write about someone else did… other people's suffering [seen] as
justified retribution… supernatural beliefs like karma might be especially
good at satisfying [self-serving motives] when other, more secular
explanations fail."
— https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2025/05/good-bad-karma — STOP 0:
people use karma to take credit and to condemn — the folk frame, measured.
Press release is public; paper itself paywalled (cite release only).

## STOP 3 — the habits bridge (science side)

**Wendy Wood, Annual Review of Psychology 2015 (QUOTED, review excerpts)** —
"habits develop… through instrumental learning… behavior becomes less
responsive to current goals and planning as habit associations strengthen"
+ "people misattribute externally-cued representations to their own natural
response… to their internal preferences and desires" (habits feel like "me")
+ habit = "a specific subset of… automaticity… triggered by a relatively
rigid form of context cuing."
— https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417 (paywalled) + open
companion PDFs at dornsife.usc.edu (Wood: Habits in Dual Process Models;
Carden & Wood 2018 — both linked from search, fetch before scripting).
STOP 3: the rational on-ramp — repetition → automatic cuing → goals lose
control. This IS perfuming in lab language. One card, no numbers invented.

## STOP 5 — the western-mirror shelf (determinism corner)

**Sapolsky × Harris #360 (2024, LINKED)** — no free will, primacy of luck,
punishment-as-tool-not-desert. The western "no judge" mirror of the film's
momentum thesis.
— https://www.samharris.org/podcasts/making-sense-episodes/360 — transcript
paywalled; LINKED only, do not quote secondhand.

**Caruso, Zygon (LINKED)** — Buddhism should adopt free-will skepticism;
forward-looking responsibility suffices. Directly supports stop 5's
"responsibility without retribution" beat.

**Finnigan, Oxford Handbook of Moral Psychology (LINKED)** — survey:
transpersonal retributive responsibility vs no-self; naturalizing karma to
character virtues; Breyer's regulation view (praise/blame to shape future
conduct, not track desert). Stop 5's courtroom, academic register.

**Repetti, J. Buddhist Ethics (LINKED)** — Buddha rejected fatalism ("would
lead to… volitional catatonia"); knowledge of cause/effect makes outcomes
evitable; mindfulness of volition = control. Stop 5's "the work works"
anchor, scholarly.

**Iso 2025, Theravāda karma ≠ determinism (LINKED, doi:10.65680/jibs.6188)**
— "karma itself does not deterministically cause external phenomena; rather
it serves as… necessary and sufficient conditions" for feelings on contact;
cave-and-stone example (don't blame karma alone when the stone, the cave
and your decision all co-caused it). Stop 3–4: karma as one condition among
many — the anti-fatalist guard INSIDE the tradition.

**Monima Chadha, Oxford Handbook (LINKED)** — karma as "moral cosmology as
well as… psychological doctrine"; karma as the tradition's "Unmoved Mover."
Stop 4's cosmic frame, one line max.
