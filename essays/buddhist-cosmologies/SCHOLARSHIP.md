## THE DIRECT ANSWER (scholarly consensus, with cites)

**Same address, different counts, three maps in Tibet.**

1. **Same Abhidharma backbone.** Encyclopedia of Buddhism (fetched):
   "The elaborate cosmological systems detailed in these two Abhidharmas
   [Theravāda + Sarvāstivāda], are, however, substantially the same,
   differing only occasionally on minor points of detail." Tibetan
   Buddhism's default map IS the Abhidharmakośa (Vasubandhu) — so the Thai
   forest 31-plane map and the Tibetan Abhidharma map describe the SAME
   Meru, SAME four continents, SAME three planes. Theravāda and Tibetan
   are two renderings of one Indian address.

2. **But the count differs: 31 vs 32/33.** Encyclopedia of Buddhism
   (fetched): "The total number of sub-divisions is between 31 and 33
   sub-realms, depending upon the tradition. The Theravada tradition
   counts 31 sub-realms in total, and the Sanskrit traditions count 32 or
   33." The discrepancies are things like whether unconscious beings
   (asañññatta) and asuras get counted as their own line. (Note: the
   "33" the owner may also be thinking of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three
   — ONE kāma-deva plane, Tāvatiṃsa, not the total.)

3. **Tibet runs THREE cosmologies at once.** Springer, *The Offering of
   Mount Meru* (2020, fetched): "The three predominant cosmologies in
   Tibet were the Abhidharma, Kālacakra, and Dzokchen (rdzogs chen)." And
   Tibet itself needed a repair manual: Khedrup Norsang Gyatso
   (1423–1513) wrote a commentary chapter titled "Resolving
   Contradictions Between Kālacakra and Abhidharma Cosmology."

4. **Kālacakra's universe is geometrically NOT Abhidharma's.** Berzin /
   StudyBuddhism (fetched, side-by-side tables):
   - Meru shape: Abhidharma = **square** cross-section, 160,000 yojanas,
     silver/blue/red/gold by direction · Kālacakra = **round**, 100,000
     yojanas, black in the east (colors shifted one position)
   - Rings: Abhidharma = **seven** golden ranges, progressively smaller ·
     Kālacakra = **six** rings, all equal
   - Form-realm heavens: Abhidharma = **seventeen** · Kālacakra =
     **sixteen**
   - A Kālacakra mile is **twice** an Abhidharma mile (~9 modern miles)
   - Hells: Abhidharma = below Bodh Gaya/Tibet · Kālacakra = inside the
     elemental mandalas
   - Sun/moon motion differs (Encyclopedia: Kālacakra "differs from
     Abhidharma texts on the shape of Mount Meru as well as on the motion
     of the celestial bodies").

5. **The scholar's answer to "how can there be two?"** — Berzin,
   *Introduction to the Kalachakra Initiation* (via Shambhala, fetched):
   "It is significant that Buddhism offers two descriptions of the
   universe. Each is valid for a different purpose, and there is no
   contradiction in having multiple portraits… Nothing exists with
   inherent characteristics on its own side rendering only one correct
   way to conventionally perceive, apprehend or describe it." Purpose-
   relative cosmology: Abhidharma's map trains discriminating awareness
   (set theory with variables); Kālacakra's is a unified field theory
   mirroring cosmos = body = mandala. "Neither was formulated for the
   sake of navigation" (Berzin, StudyBuddhism).

6. **Asura: the five-vs-six fight is the counting fight.** Samye
   Institute (fetched): "Some early Indian Buddhist schools counted only
   five classes as they counted the asuras as members of the god realm.
   In the Tibetan tradition, over time it has become more common to list
   six classes." And: "The status of asuras is not consistent across all
   texts" — some texts put asuras in the lower realms instead. The
   Theravāda 31-plane count has asuras NOT as a separate plane; the
   Tibetan bhavacakra makes them one of six. (Wikipedia claims the
   six-world asura split is "Tsongkhapa's authority" — NEEDS A BETTER
   SOURCE before banking as fact.)

7. **The "mental" reading is stated by the traditions themselves.**
   Samye Institute (fetched): "In the Mahayana teachings, the hell realms
   (like all the other realms) are understood to be mindstates that are
   characterized by a particular type of suffering, rather than actual
   physical locations." — tradition-side psychologization, quotable.
