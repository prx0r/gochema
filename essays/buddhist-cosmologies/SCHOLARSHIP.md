## THE DIRECT ANSWER (scholarly consensus, with cites)

**Same address, different counts, three maps in Tibet.**

1. **Same Abhidharma backbone.** Encyclopedia of Buddhism (fetched):
   "The elaborate cosmological systems detailed in these two Abhidharmas
   [Theravāda + Sarvāstivāda], are, however, substantially the same,
   differing only occasionally on minor points of detail." Tibetan
   Buddhism's default map IS the Abhidharmakośa (Vasubandhu) — so the Thai
   forest 31-plane map and the Tibetan Abhidharma map describe the SAME
   Meru, SAME four continents, SAME three planes. Theravāda and Tibetan
   are two renderings of one Indian address.

2. **But the count differs: 31 vs 32/33.** Encyclopedia of Buddhism
   (fetched): "The total number of sub-divisions is between 31 and 33
   sub-realms, depending upon the tradition. The Theravada tradition
   counts 31 sub-realms in total, and the Sanskrit traditions count 32 or
   33." The discrepancies are things like whether unconscious beings
   (asañññatta) and asuras get counted as their own line. (Note: the
   "33" the owner may also be thinking of the Heaven of the Thirty-Three
   — ONE kāma-deva plane, Tāvatiṃsa, not the total.)

3. **Tibet runs THREE cosmologies at once.** Springer, *The Offering of
   Mount Meru* (2020, fetched): "The three predominant cosmologies in
   Tibet were the Abhidharma, Kālacakra, and Dzokchen (rdzogs chen)." And
   Tibet itself needed a repair manual: Khedrup Norsang Gyatso
   (1423–1513) wrote a commentary chapter titled "Resolving
   Contradictions Between Kālacakra and Abhidharma Cosmology."

4. **Kālacakra's universe is geometrically NOT Abhidharma's.** Berzin /
   StudyBuddhism (fetched, side-by-side tables):
   - Meru shape: Abhidharma = **square** cross-section, 160,000 yojanas,
     silver/blue/red/gold by direction · Kālacakra = **round**, 100,000
     yojanas, black in the east (colors shifted one position)
   - Rings: Abhidharma = **seven** golden ranges, progressively smaller ·
     Kālacakra = **six** rings, all equal
   - Form-realm heavens: Abhidharma = **seventeen** · Kālacakra =
     **sixteen**
   - A Kālacakra mile is **twice** an Abhidharma mile (~9 modern miles)
   - Hells: Abhidharma = below Bodh Gaya/Tibet · Kālacakra = inside the
     elemental mandalas
   - Sun/moon motion differs (Encyclopedia: Kālacakra "differs from
     Abhidharma texts on the shape of Mount Meru as well as on the motion
     of the celestial bodies").

5. **The scholar's answer to "how can there be two?"** — Berzin,
   *Introduction to the Kalachakra Initiation* (via Shambhala, fetched):
   "It is significant that Buddhism offers two descriptions of the
   universe. Each is valid for a different purpose, and there is no
   contradiction in having multiple portraits… Nothing exists with
   inherent characteristics on its own side rendering only one correct
   way to conventionally perceive, apprehend or describe it." Purpose-
   relative cosmology: Abhidharma's map trains discriminating awareness
   (set theory with variables); Kālacakra's is a unified field theory
   mirroring cosmos = body = mandala. "Neither was formulated for the
   sake of navigation" (Berzin, StudyBuddhism).

6. **Asura: the five-vs-six fight is the counting fight.** Samye
   Institute (fetched): "Some early Indian Buddhist schools counted only
   five classes as they counted the asuras as members of the god realm.
   In the Tibetan tradition, over time it has become more common to list
   six classes." And: "The status of asuras is not consistent across all
   texts" — some texts put asuras in the lower realms instead. The
   Theravāda 31-plane count has asuras NOT as a separate plane; the
   Tibetan bhavacakra makes them one of six. (Wikipedia claims the
   six-world asura split is "Tsongkhapa's authority" — NEEDS A BETTER
   SOURCE before banking as fact.)

7. **The "mental" reading is stated by the traditions themselves.**
   Samye Institute (fetched): "In the Mahayana teachings, the hell realms
   (like all the other realms) are understood to be mindstates that are
   characterized by a particular type of suffering, rather than actual
   physical locations." — tradition-side psychologization, quotable.

## THE INSTRUMENT LEG (CH.11 sources — science side, fetched 2026-10-21)

**[S3] Wheeler, J.A. — "Information, Physics, Quantum: The Search for
Links" (1989/1990) — QUOTE FETCHED from the paper PDF (philpapers.org/
archive/WHEIPQ.pdf):**
"No element in the description of physics shows itself as closer to
primordial than the elementary quantum phenomenon, that is, the
elementary device-intermediated act of posing a yes-no physical question
and eliciting an answer … Otherwise stated, every physical quantity,
every it, derives its ultimate significance from bits, binary yes-or-no
indications, a conclusion which we epitomize in the phrase, it from bit."
— Fuller formulation (widely reprinted, Wheeler & Ford): "every it —
every particle, every field of force, even the spacetime continuum itself
— derives its function, its meaning, its very existence entirely — even
if in some contexts indirectly — from the apparatus-elicited answers to
yes or no questions, binary choices, bits."
— ROLE in CH.11 beat 3: science pushed to its floor arrives at INFORMATION
(an answer to a question) as the most granular thing — and answers require
the asking. The instrument is inside the result. Owner's "that's the most
granular you can get" = Wheeler's own claim.
— Guard note: Wheeler = participatory physicalism, NOT Buddhism. The
parting (beat 4): canon claims liberation-from-the-sensorium, Wheeler
claims participation-in-it. Don't fuse them.

**[S4] Berzin, Alexander — Kālacakra body-measures + clear-light ground —
QUOTES FETCHED (studybuddhism.com Abhidharma-vs-Kalachakra + shambhala.com
"Shape of the Universe"):**
- Body-isomorphism: "From the bottom of the wind mandala to the peak of
  Mount Meru is 400,000 yojanas. This parallels the measurement from the
  bottom of the feet to the top of the head being four cubits… Mount Meru
  being 100,000 yojanas parallels … the spine being one cubit. The neck of
  Mount Meru being 25,000 yojanas parallels the neck of the human body."
- Same-shape summary (Shambhala): "the proportions and shape of the
  universe, human body, and the mandala and body of the Buddha-figure
  Kalachakra are all the same."
- Ground (Shambhala): "A description of the external and internal worlds
  in terms of their unifying parallels reveals the shared underlying basis
  from which both derive, namely, clear light mind. The winds of karma
  that provide the impulses for a particular universe to evolve come from
  the collective karma on the clear light minds of prior beings."
- Not-for-navigation (studybuddhism): "neither was formulated for the
  sake of navigation."
— ROLE: CH.11 beat 2 is the tradition saying "awareness is the ground
truth, body is the doorway" IN ITS OWN WORDS with numbers attached.

**[S9] Lion's Roar — "Making Sense of Buddhism's Six Realms" (2026, fetched):**
- The sobering definition (CH.2 beat A): "samsara is not where we exist —
  it's how we exist. Samsara isn't a location on any cosmic map. It's a
  state of delusion. And if samsara is how rather than where, then the six
  realms aren't destinations either. They're modes of experiencing."
- The two-camps frame (CH.2 beat C): "Most of us respond in one of two
  ways. We either accept a watered-down interpretation — treating the
  realms as mere metaphors for psychological states — or we politely set
  aside these teachings as cultural artifacts… Both approaches let us
  continue practicing without cognitive dissonance."
- The mode cadence (CH.7 beat A): "Moment by moment, we move from the
  bliss of the god realms to the anger of the hell realms to the stupidity
  of the animal realm."
- Genre note: modern dharma journalism — VOICE = witness, not primary.

## THE SOURCE-LAYER LEG (CH.3 — what the Buddha assumed/refused, fetched 2026-10-21)

**[S16] Kotyk, Jeffrey — "Abhidharmakośa as a Reflection of Earlier Āgama
and Nikāya Buddhism" (UBC, 2022 — free PDF, fetched excerpts):**
- "The world of Mt. Sumeru and the Four Continents is the foundational
  worldview of the Buddhists in ancient India. Abhidharma inherited the
  cosmology of early Buddhism."
- "This cosmology is attributed to the Buddha himself. It was conceived of
  as physical and not figurative or mythological."
- "The physical world is brought about due to the collective karma of all
  beings… it is not organized according to some plan or divine willpower.
  There is no Creator in the Buddhist worldview."
- "Vasubandhu elaborates upon an earlier ancient model of Buddhist
  cosmology while remaining faithful to it."
— ROLE: CH.3 beats B/D — the physically-real reading of the source layer
+ the "no creator, collective-kamma" engine + the inheritance chain
(early → Abhidharma).
